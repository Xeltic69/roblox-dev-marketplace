# Case study: a simulator launch post-mortem (one creator, unaudited)

A creator who shipped three viral games described the third, an egg-steal and pet-raising simulator modeled on a popular egg-stealing game. It peaked during an admin-abuse event, earned noticeably less than its projected revenue (the headline figure was extrapolated from one weekend), and was effectively dead after about a month. These are anecdotal heuristics from one team, not platform rules. Validate against your own analytics. General lessons live in the topic files; this page keeps the case-specific details.

## What happened
- **Onboarding:** the title said "steal the egg", so the largest funnel drop was the first steal step (the title/first-minute mismatch is the lesson, see [onboarding](onboarding-retention-ux.md)). Fixes: auto-equip the carried egg, pre-seeded food so the player can feed immediately, teach where food comes from only when the stock runs out, a first feed that grows the pet about five times for a clear "wow", and contextual tutorials fired by triggers (cash at a threshold, first full plot, out of consumables).
- **Progression wall:** launch D1 was in the single digits and roughly doubled after fixing a wall in minutes 5-10. The creator had tested only on a progressed account; milestone funnels showed that only part of the players bought the first core upgrade and fewer upgraded their plot. Paid testers logged a multi-hour grind with item, cost, time and stuck points, which fed balancing changes ([iteration and playtesting](iteration-liveops-playtesting.md)).
- **Monetization:** whale products (a high-priced limited item with a fixed stock) shipped two weeks late; ARPDAU roughly doubled once whale sinks existed and recovered after a mobile shop fix. About half the players were female at peak, and a cute limited sold well. Regrets: selling cash packs, overpowered paid items, and giving away too much during admin events ([monetization playbook](monetization-playbook.md)). Prices here are the creator's, not a recommendation.
- **Feedback and testing:** an in-world mailbox for feedback (summarize it into themes), a dedicated tester group before launch, and a published test place. Daily patching was normal; the regret was launching before testers had found economy-breaking exploits (one tester generated absurd amounts of in-game cash).
- **Production:** open creative freedom produced three clashing art styles across about eight modelers and a few hundred dollars of unusable, AI-generated, badly rigged models. Give an art-style guide and references up front; part ways quickly with contractors who miss deadlines ([team and production](team-production-mindset.md)).

## Strategic caveat
The creator was leaving simulators because their lifespan was about a month and AI-assisted clones crowd the market. Treat trend simulators as short-lived, plan LiveOps cadence and economy longevity ([release and LiveOps](release-liveops.md)), or invest in more durable original concepts.
