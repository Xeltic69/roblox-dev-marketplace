# Market gaps, fantasy, and creator evidence

This route supports ideation, concept comparison, or a requested growth diagnosis. It does not replace the user's chosen game with a trend merely because another game has more players.

## Five-question checklist

1. **Recognizable demand:** is the idea relatable/clickable, or does the fantasy have an engaged audience? Continued activity in neglected competitors can be a lead, but not proof of an underserved market.
2. **Shareable play:** can players describe a memorable moment to friends, cooperate/compete meaningfully, or capture a clear short clip? Test the mechanic, not just a hypothetical thumbnail.
3. **Established mechanics:** which working interaction patterns can transfer to this fantasy, and what makes them effective? Reuse principles, not another developer's assets, branding, code, or protected expression.
4. **Meaningful difference:** what unmet need does the twist serve? Changing a mostly PvE fantasy to PvP may create a distinct audience, but also adds griefing, fairness, and networking costs.
5. **Off-platform evidence and comprehension:** has a related loop attracted players elsewhere, and can the target Roblox audience understand and control the adaptation? Demand does not guarantee successful translation.

Treat these as a research checklist from a top-developer consultation relayed by a creator, not a prediction formula or required genre. There is no universal "20% twist" metric or guaranteed viral feature list.

## Evidence worksheet

For each candidate, record fantasy, target audience, core loop, existing alternatives, observed unmet need, dated demand signal, proposed difference, implementation risks, and smallest useful validation. Keep observations separate from inference. Cite the actual competitor/game ID and time window rather than "the market loves it."

Use [RoTrends](https://www.rotrends.com/) as one third-party observation source. Record its displayed timestamps, rankings, concurrent activity, update age, and metric labels when accessible. A rank is relative; a snapshot is not a trend. An estimated session duration is not verified retention, and an earnings rank is not audited revenue. Cross-check with direct experience inspection, current Roblox discovery documentation, player feedback, and prototype observations. Do not scrape aggressively or infer hidden dashboards.

Compare several independent signals: persistent activity over time, public demand for a fantasy, understandable off-platform mechanics, competitor complaints, and prototype comprehension. Account for update spikes, advertising, seasonality, copied audiences, and survivorship bias. Absence of a competitor may mean difficulty or low demand rather than opportunity. Avoid fake certainty from an additive score; document reasons for prioritization and disconfirming evidence.

## Creator material

Use reputable, topic-appropriate creator content as an additional layer. Evaluate concrete examples, experience relevant to the claim, reproducibility, dates, transparent corrections, and incentives. Popularity is not technical authority. Trace the actual account and check dates and claims yourself. Producer and design case discussions from creators are useful hypotheses, not an engine API reference or independently audited business evidence.

For each creator source you rely on, record title, URL, date, the idea used, how you verified it and its limitation. A transcript or caption mirror can contain errors; a title or search snippet is not evidence of what was said.

Useful creator-derived hypotheses include looking beyond Roblox for understandable loops, connecting actions to a meaningful fantasy, and separating urgent fixes from deliberate content updates. Validate them in the user's game. Do not import promises of revenue/virality, manipulative spending advice, numerical success laws, or an inflexible production order. Keep quotation and reuse within source permissions; write an independent synthesis.
